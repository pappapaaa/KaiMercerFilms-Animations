# Outlines and materials

This report uses decoded pixels from the four supplied shorts. See [COLOR_AND_LIGHTING.md](COLOR_AND_LIGHTING.md) for sampling details, palette families, native color patches, and the complete 779-frame dense-sheet coverage log for `XdcSli5STR4`.

## The observed ink hierarchy

The strongest contour belongs to the character silhouette. Eyes, mouth edges, fingers, clothing folds, and expression marks generally use finer lines. Background construction lines are finer again and are frequently colored or softened. Soft cloud edges in the blue-sky gag have no comparable black perimeter. Applying a single equally heavy outline pass to every mesh would therefore flatten the hierarchy and overemphasize the room.

Contour width grows with the size of the depicted subject. A close-up head can have a black core over twenty pixels wide at 1080 output width, while a small figure's silhouette can have a core around six pixels. These examples do not imply one constant source stroke width: drawings, camera framing, scale, and shape orientation all matter.

## Reproducible native-pixel profiles

The measurement is a perpendicular single scan through a visually chosen clean edge. A pixel belongs to the dark core when `max(R,G,B) < 45`. Width is the length of that contiguous run; antialiasing fringe and nearby lighter shadow pixels are excluded. It is a conservative black-core width, not the complete perceptual stroke width. Scan ranges and cropped 4× nearest-neighbor magnifications are generated by [measure_profiles.py](../look-measurements/measure_profiles.py); raw results are [profiles.json](../look-measurements/profiles.json).

## Edge behavior across the full Shotgun/Dogs short

The thirteen inspected dense pages cover f0–778 without gaps. The change from the cockpit wide to pilot close-up at f88 keeps the same rank order: strong character edges, thinner clothing lines, still finer panel/control marks. The cloud wide at f302 uses crisp figures and podium but soft unoutlined clouds. The reaction close-up at f438 has strong face contours and thin eye/eyebrow marks. The tight crop at f629 enlarges the contour with the subject. These are separate drawings/compositions; do not infer a global post-process edge thickness from their appearance.

In the cockpit, realistic-looking control density is still illustration: small toggles, panels, and seats have flat graphic surfaces and restrained highlights. In the cloud sequence, a stylized golden halo and podium ornament do not require metal reflections. The material identity is conveyed by shape, flat color, a small highlight/shadow division, and context.

## Material vocabulary

| Visible surface | Evidence | Reusable implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Pale/white face and body | TikTok f240 white patch has zero RGB variation; adjacent cool-blue patch is also uniform | Painted/unlit front color plus deliberate shadow region; keep facial features above the color layer |
| Colored body shadow | TikTok f240 `#b9d4fd`; blue side shapes recur in Shotgun/Dogs | One designed secondary tone; avoid a noisy gradient of physical light across the face |
| Warm flat backdrop | Refrenced f48 `#f5b393`, zero patch variation | Large clean solid shape, optionally a very gentle background-only gradient |
| Night interior | Christmas f336 dark ceiling and varying illuminated wall patch | Broad baked light pools or a few clean planes; preserve the brightest face/prop shapes |
| Illustrated wood/card | Brown desk/podium, thin seams, restrained broad shading | Flat warm base, darker side, a few structural strokes; no dense photographic grain |
| Sky and clouds | Shotgun/Dogs f336 upper/lower sky medians differ; soft pale clouds | Broad vertical sky gradient plus original soft-edged cloud layers |
| Background wear | Sparse scratches and irregular marks visible in room/panel art | Static seeded marks at low contrast, concentrated where meaningful |
| Glass/window | Cyan background with a few diagonal pale reflections in cockpit | One translucent-looking painted band or simple graphic sheen; avoid full environment reflections |

There is no positive evidence here for a universal grain overlay, physically based roughness map, ambient-occlusion preset, metalness value, or photoreal texture. These cannot be estimated reliably from flat compressed drawings. The public videos also do not reveal the original authoring software.

## Translating the principles into the original Three.js template

[Design.ts](../../templates/bridge/Design.ts) centralizes palettes and **proposed** starting values. `INK.characterOuterToHeadWidth=.022`, `characterInnerToOuter=.44`, `backgroundPx=2.5`, and `propPx=3.5` are an implementation guide; they are not statistical estimates of all source strokes. Judge a representative close-up and a wide shot at the actual export size. At 1440 output width, a desired 1080-width pixel target scales by 1440/1080, but object-space contours also change with camera distance.

[Props.ts](../../templates/bridge/Props.ts) provides `outlinedBox()` with darker edge cylinders. They create stable object-space line work for simple prop blocks. This is a pragmatic construction for an original shallow scene, not a reconstruction of how the source made its outlines. Radius is in world units, so it must be checked under the intended camera. Avoid adding a second global outline pass that doubles those edges.

The parcel's blank paper label and center tape read as simple graphic shapes. The plant uses a few extruded original leaf shapes, not dense foliage. Background `MeshBasicMaterial` planes retain the painted palette. `MeshToonMaterial` on shallow boxes/leaves gives mild real volume under the bridge's lights without requiring reflective materials.

[Paint.ts](../../templates/bridge/Paint.ts) makes deterministic procedural wall/floor art. Its thin seams and sparse marks support the environment. The base color carries most of the image. The seed must remain stable across frames; do not use wall-clock randomness in a renderer.

## Depth and shading choices

The user requested future characters with a little body depth. That can be achieved with a shallow extrusion, a modest bevel, a subdued side tone, and a contact shadow. Proposed defaults in `DEPTH` are body depth .12 units, head depth .10, bevel .035, contact-shadow opacity .18, no room fog, and zero default blur. These values are original design decisions. They do not imply a matching physical scale in the reference.

Favor the readable drawn front plane. A broad shiny highlight, spherical face gradient, deeply modeled nose, thick visible side wall, or aggressive ambient occlusion moves toward a different character language. Face and hand silhouettes should be approved at the intended camera angle before adding depth. For details of the actor implementation, use [CHARACTERS.md](CHARACTERS.md) and [DEPTH_AND_STAGING.md](DEPTH_AND_STAGING.md).

For a new prop, define its silhouette and story purpose first. Build a small number of color masses, assign a darker side only where it clarifies the form, add the necessary contour, and test it at phone size. A parcel needs tape and a readable block shape; it does not need corrugation, realistic shipping text, or dozens of beveled seams.

## Common failure modes and remedies

| Failure in a new render | Likely cause | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Character looks like shiny plastic | Specular highlights/physical gradient dominate | Flatten front material; use deliberate side tone and mild bevel |
| Room competes with face | Equal black outline everywhere or excessive texture | Thin/color background lines; lower background contrast |
| Eyes merge into black blobs | Feature widths/spacing not reviewed at output size | Open spacing and reduce inner line weight; inspect native still |
| Prop edge weight changes badly between shots | One object-space radius used over a huge camera range | Author shot-aware scale or alternate prop detail level; review both framings |
| Outlines double up | Geometry outlines plus a global edge pass | Choose one primary mechanism per object |
| Tiny objects shimmer | Subpixel lines or over-detailed panels | Remove detail, widen stable marks, or use a painted background layer |
| Held frames crawl | Time-based procedural texture, random light, or auto-exposure | Seed textures once; use deterministic frame-indexed motion only |
| Softness obscures expressions | Full-scene blur used to mimic a soft foreground | Separate foreground/background softness; keep actor facial plane crisp |

## Validation scope

The evidence establishes visible color/ink relationships and a small set of native-pixel measurements. It does not recover proprietary project settings. The template is an original reusable adaptation with new sets and props. Validate it with original test actions and representative stills; copying a source frame is not required to test these general visual principles.
