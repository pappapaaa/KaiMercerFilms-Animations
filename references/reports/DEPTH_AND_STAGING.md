## Reconstruction recipe for the template

1. Build a small layer hierarchy: background wash or painted wall, architecture, characters/props, selected foreground occluder. Keep depth ranges sufficient to avoid z-fighting but do not add unnecessary perspective distortion.
2. Use a modest perspective camera for a dimensional stage. For a flatter dialogue stage, increase camera distance and reduce field of view, or use the supplied orthographic framing option if available. Exact focal length cannot be recovered from these clips.
3. Give props only the planes needed for their role. A box or desk needs a visible top and side; a postcard can stay a card with an ink edge. Preserve hand contact and prop scale.
4. Keep faces graphic and close to a frontal plane. The user-requested shallow body thickness should be visible mostly in three-quarter views. Start with character depth 0.12 against height 2.40 (~5% of height); adjust visually.
5. Use broad shadow shapes, soft contact shadows, and modest side-lighting. They should explain form without turning the scene into glossy plastic.
6. Use depth blur selectively: strongly softened foreground in the Christmas overhead scene is an observed device, but Refrenced's desktop stage needs clean graphic edges. Do not apply the same heavy blur to every shot.
7. Frame start, middle, and end states. An intentional foreground crop is allowed; accidental loss of the acting face, a hand-object contact, or the whole joke is not.

## Occlusion and geometry rules

- Ground at y=0; character front points +Z. Demo actor is approximately 2.4 units high.
- The character's caller owns root position/rotation. `update()` resets local pose and is deterministic for backward/random seeks.
- Place held props under a hand/attachment anchor, or derive their world position from it. Visual proximity alone does not establish a grip.
- Mouth/eye planes should sit just ahead of the head cap, with transparent depth writing disabled and stable render order. Check both oblique views for floating decals.
- Use explicit front/side shape ordering for overlapping limbs. A dark seam across a bent arm should be a deliberate drawing line, not an accidental duplicate outline.
- Contact shadows should remain close to feet or prop bases. Large offset blobs can imply hovering.

### The implemented sleeve overlap

The preview revealed dotted dark arcs where extruded sleeve surfaces occupied nearly the same depth. The rig now separates the caps using offsets proportional to `bodyDepth`, so turning the depth control does not reintroduce the same coincidence. With `d = bodyDepth`, the upper sleeve center is `−0.50d`, the elbow/forearm center is `+0.40d` in character coordinates, and the hand center is `+0.62d`. The tunic front cap is `+0.625d`; the forearm front cap is `+0.875d`. Thus the upper sleeve can disappear cleanly behind the torso while the forearm can pass in front. The overlapping shapes remain editable and the joint does not depend on a screen-space hiding patch.

These are proposed implementation layers, not recovered source geometry. Inspect oblique views when adding a new gesture; large side turns may require a dedicated three-quarter drawing or a more volumetric arm rig.

## What the references do not establish

They do not establish true mesh thickness, precise lens values, light wattage, depth-of-field aperture, or a single universal character rig. The source combines flat graphic staging, perspective backgrounds, scale gags, and dramatic soft layers. Our TypeScript/Three.js implementation is one reproducible way to capture those visible qualities, with a configurable shallow-volume adaptation requested by the user.
